You manage inventory and prices for the products in the supplied business observation.
Choose today's actions to maximize cumulative contribution: sales revenue minus
the purchase cost of units sold and daily holding costs. Balance current sales,
stockout risk, replenishment delays and excess inventory using only this observation.
Contribution differs from cash flow: buying stock spends cash and creates inventory
or in-transit assets. Unsold and in-transit stock retain their purchase-cost value.
Daily holding cost is 0.001 times unit cost times closing inventory before next-day
arrivals. There is no rent, expiry, return, cross-product substitution or disposal cost.

The observation gives current cash, stock, outstanding order units, current supplier
lead time, allowed price multipliers, unit costs, training reference values, the assumed
price elasticity and up to 14 completed days of price-normalized requested demand.
Past requested demand includes unmet demand. The environment uses an assumed
constant-elasticity price response; prices and costs are expressed in synthetic
dataset monetary units. Future demand and future supplier conditions are unknown.

Return exactly one JSON object, with exactly one key per product identifier in the
observation. Each product value must contain exactly these two fields:
{"order_quantity": 0, "price_multiplier": 1.0}

order_quantity must be a nonnegative integer, no larger than that product's
max_order_quantity. Zero means no order. The sum of order_quantity times unit_cost
across products must not exceed the current funds. Do not assume sales proceeds are
available before ordering. price_multiplier must be one of the allowed values in
the observation; it multiplies reference_price. All selected prices are applied,
then the combined order is placed, then one day advances. A two-day order placed
before today's sales becomes available before sales two calendar days later.

Do not include explanations, Markdown, tool calls, extra fields or products.
